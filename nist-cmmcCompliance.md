# NIST 800-171 & CMMC 2.0: Strategic Compliance for Executives

Organizations handling Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI) must safeguard it adequately to comply with federal regulations. NIST Special Publication 800-171 outlines standards and controls to protect CUI within non-federal information systems. The Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification (CMMC) 2.0 builds on these requirements by validating NIST 800-171 adherence.

## Strategic Business Impact for Executive Leadership

Achieving NIST 800-171 and CMMC 2.0 compliance is not solely a technical challenge—it's a leadership priority. Executive stakeholders drive the success of compliance programs by aligning cybersecurity objectives with business strategy, allocating resources, and fostering a culture of continuous improvement.

### CTOs/CIOs: Technology Strategy Alignment

## Security and Compliance Stakeholders

Effective compliance requires coordination between multiple stakeholders. While executives provide strategic direction, operational leaders and technical teams must execute control implementations, maintain documentation, and support audits. Key stakeholder groups include:

- **Executive Leadership**: Establish governance, allocate resources, define risk appetite.
- **IT & Security Teams**: Operationalize controls, manage risk, enforce zero-trust in incident response. Continuous observability and montiroing are required for today's organizations.
- **Compliance Officers**: Monitor adherence to NIST/CMMC controls and oversee audits.
- **Third-Party Vendors**: Ensure service providers and technologies meet security standards like SOC 2.

## Understanding the Relationship Between NIST 800-171 and CMMC 2.0

CMMC 2.0 Level 2 certification requirements align directly with NIST 800-171. This allows organizations to follow a unified compliance strategy and reduce cost and complexity by addressing both frameworks concurrently.

## Developing the System Security Plan (SSP)

An SSP should define boundaries, environments, roles, and control implementations. It must include diagrams, inventories, and procedures with scheduled updates to reflect current operational states.

## Plan of Action & Milestones (POAM)

The POAM documents compliance gaps, remediation plans, responsibilities, and timelines. An updated POAM showcases continuous security posture improvement.

## Integrating the CAPA Framework

Corrective Action & Preventive Action (CAPA) supports long-term compliance readiness. It identifies root causes, implements fixes, and reinforces processes through governance, resourcing, and measurement.
